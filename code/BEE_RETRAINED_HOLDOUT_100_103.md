# Frozen retrained-probe evaluation on seeds 100–103

The [evaluation-only Kaggle notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/faithfulchukwunwogor/beehive-retrained-probe-holdout) is `beehive_retrained_probe_holdout.ipynb`, generated by `build_bee_retrained_holdout_notebook.py`. Its [smoke version](https://www.kaggle.com/code/faithfulchukwunwogor/beehive-retrained-probe-holdout-smoke) checks the pipeline on a short run. This experiment loads the saved 14-output probe and its layer-13/14 weight matrices, fixed signed attribute directions, learned nonnegative weights, and steering strength **0.045** from the prior retraining run. The Qwen3.5-0.8B bee LoRA and the simulator remain the same. No probe training, mask fitting, direction recalibration, or strength selection occurs in this notebook.

The prior experiment did not save the two activation-norm scalars used to scale the injection. This notebook reconstructs them by replaying exactly the same four source trajectories and targeted counterfactual examples, then computing neutral teacher-forced activations on the same 2,623 training examples. It verifies the example-kind counts and zero-error source replay, saves the recovered scalars, and uses them without fitting new probe weights. The smoke subset checks the code path; the full notebook uses the complete original example set.

Final seeds **100–103** each run baseline, equal signed steering, and learned-weight signed steering for two simulated hours. Each arm has 50 bees, 15-minute rounds, batches of up to 16 bees sharing one frozen local world view, and 400 action-level 14-component hive-state snapshots. The vector remains researcher-only. The notebook exports paired final scores, all per-seed vectors, the timestamped immediate/background/total step-change transcript, and an overlaid mean score plot.

Four paired seeds provide more evidence about whether the small two-seed gain persists. Seeds 100–103 were used in prior experiments with other probe directions, so they are additional evaluation seeds for the retrained intervention, but not wholly untouched study seeds. A comparison against baseline on these seeds is valid; any claim that the new directions outperform the old directions also needs attention to the strength difference between experiments.

## Smoke and full deployment

The smoke notebook completed in **8 minutes 48 seconds**. All four source arms replayed with zero vector error. The smoke subset reconstructed both activation norms, ran baseline/equal/learned steering for 24 actions each, and saved 72 bounded vector records and an overlaid plot with zero schema-invalid decisions. Smoke norms are subset-only functional checks; the full notebook reconstructs the norms from all 2,623 original examples.

The full notebook completed in **2 hours 10 seconds**, within the pre-run estimate of 2–2½ hours after worker start. The complete source replay reconstructed activation norms of **1.8094291687** at layer 13 and **1.7995562553** at layer 14. There was no probe or weight fitting during this run.

Learned steering improved the final score on **three of four seeds**; equal steering improved **two of four**. At the end of hour one, the mean paired gains were +0.000206587 for equal steering and +0.000607531 for learned steering. At the end of hour two, they were +0.000310136 and +0.000838722, respectively. The learned advantage persisted and grew in this four-seed mean, although seed 101 declined slightly.

Mean final attributes across the four seeds show where the learned arm changed the system. The largest improvement was brood nutrition (+0.019825 versus baseline). Structural integrity (+0.002637) and hygiene (+0.002175) also improved. Adult energy sufficiency (−0.004902), pollen reserve (−0.004406), carbohydrate reserve (−0.003024), and thermal suitability (−0.000899) declined. The learned arm chose `feed_brood` 27 times across four seeds versus 16 in baseline, which is consistent with the brood gain and pollen cost. These are observed associations, not an isolated causal attribution for each attribute.

## Verification and interpretation

The exported transcript contains **4,800 action-level snapshots**: 12 arms × 400 actions, each with 14 finite values in [0, 1]. Sequential event IDs, score arithmetic, and immediate/background/total step changes reconcile with the snapshots. All arms had zero schema-invalid actions. The four baseline runs reproduced their earlier baseline final vectors exactly, supporting that the world and policy were unchanged. The full audit found zero errors.

This is stronger evidence than the preceding two-seed result for a modest learned-steering gain, but it is not conclusive: four seeds give a small sample, seeds 100–103 appeared in previous experiments, and intervention selection was informed by earlier screens. The older probe's learned steering had a mean paired effect of −0.000129 on these same seeds, compared with +0.000839 here, but the older run used strength **0.03**, so this comparison does not isolate the effect of retraining the probe. The result supports further testing on genuinely untouched seeds with a predeclared intervention.
